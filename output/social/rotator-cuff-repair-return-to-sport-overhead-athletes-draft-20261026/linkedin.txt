Clearing an overhead athlete after rotator cuff repair on a calendar date answers only half the question.

Time since surgery, repair size and the surgeon's protocol tell you what the tissue can tolerate. They do not tell you what the athlete can actually do. That second question is a measurement problem, and it is one a clinic can answer.

The 2022 Bern consensus statement frames shoulder return to sport as a staged, criteria-led process. Progression depends on meeting function and capacity targets, not elapsed time alone. The evidence base is smaller than for the ACL, so the criteria need to come from data you collect yourself.

At baseline, record the surgeon's findings (tear size, tissue quality, associated pathology), then measure what you can influence: strength against normative expectation using a handheld dynamometer, the Patient-Specific Functional Scale for sport tasks, and a three-dimensional pain score (frequency, intensity, duration).

Takeaway: define the target sport movement first, then set measurable criteria in the same position and protocol at every visit.
