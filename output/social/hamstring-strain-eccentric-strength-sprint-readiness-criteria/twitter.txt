[1/6] Hamstring strains have one of sports medicine's worst recurrence rates. Most of that failure comes down to one untested variable at discharge. Here's what the evidence says.

---

[2/6] Most clinics clear hamstring strains on time elapsed, pain-free range and subjective confidence. Eccentric strength at long muscle length, the position sprinting stresses most, is rarely measured.

---

[3/6] A systematic review and meta-analysis found eccentric knee flexor deficits persist in a meaningful proportion of athletes months after they're cleared to play (Maniar et al., 2016). Clearance isn't the same as full strength recovery.

---

[4/6] Sprinting loads the hamstring eccentrically in late swing, near its longest functional length. Reviews of return-to-play link recurrence to strength symmetry and load progression, not days since injury (Fournier-Farley et al., 2016).

---

[5/6] No isokinetic rig needed. A standardised Nordic hamstring curl protocol with a fixed ankle anchor gives clinics a repeatable field measure of eccentric strength deficit.
