A 4-month post-op mark tells you nothing about whether a shoulder can decelerate a 90mph serve.

Most rotator cuff repair protocols are built for the general population: a pain-free shoulder for daily tasks. Overhead athletes need to produce and absorb force at end-range, repeatedly, under fatigue. That's why return-to-sport rates in this group consistently lag behind return-to-any-activity rates.

The 2022 Bern Consensus Statement is explicit: return-to-sport should be criteria-based, not timeline-based, because tissue healing and functional readiness don't run on the same clock (Schwank et al., 2022). Tear size, retraction grade, fatty infiltration, patient age and concomitant labral pathology all shape the trajectory and should be documented at baseline, not treated as background history.

Static strength symmetry is necessary but not sufficient. It misses rate of force development, fatigue tolerance and eccentric deceleration capacity, all of which matter more on court or on the mound.

Build return-to-sport decisions around criteria, not the calendar.
