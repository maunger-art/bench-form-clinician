Shoulder outcome measures are only as defensible as the measurement process underneath them. Most PROMs used in athletic rehab were validated in general orthopaedic populations, so athletes can score near the ceiling while lacking capacity for sport-specific demands. Reliability problems compound this: physical examination tests for shoulder pathology show wide variation depending on rater and protocol. Standardised, repeatable testing should come before questionnaire selection, with self-report and performance measures used together rather than interchangeably.
