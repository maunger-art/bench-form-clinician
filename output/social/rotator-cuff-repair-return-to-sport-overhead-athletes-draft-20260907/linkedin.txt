Time-based rotator cuff repair protocols are guesswork dressed up as a plan.

An overhead athlete returning from rotator cuff repair is a different clinical problem to a sedentary patient regaining pain-free function. The repaired tendon has to tolerate thousands of high-velocity rotational loads a season, not just get through daily life without pain.

Tear size, retraction grade, fatty infiltration and tissue quality at the time of repair all set the ceiling on strength recovery. The 2022 Bern Consensus Statement argues for individualised, criteria-based rehab frameworks over generic timelines, precisely because outcomes vary this much between athletes and sports (Schwank et al., 2022).

Fixed return points, back to throwing at 16 weeks, back to competition at six months, ignore this. Criteria-based progression, where athletes advance on strength and functional markers rather than elapsed weeks, has already outperformed time-based return in other tissue-injury populations (Mendiguchia et al., 2017; Stojanović et al., 2023).

Practical takeaway: log tear size, retraction grade and tissue quality at intake, then gate every progression decision on measured strength trends against age- and sex-matched norms, not the calendar.
