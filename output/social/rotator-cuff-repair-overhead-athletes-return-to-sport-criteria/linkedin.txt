**Most return-to-sport decisions after rotator cuff repair in overhead athletes still rest on a protocol, a manual muscle test and a feeling that the shoulder looks ready.**

The evidence does not support that confidence. Shoulder return-to-sport research is far smaller than the ACL literature, and high-quality trials specific to cuff repair in overhead athletes are scarce.

The 2022 Bern consensus frames return to sport as a continuum and supports clinical and functional criteria to guide progression. It is a consensus position, not a validated set of cut-offs. Reviews of SLAP tears and anterior instability show the same gap: readiness criteria vary widely between studies.

One trap is comparing with the other side. In overhead athletes the dominant arm is often adapted by years of repetitive loading, so it is an unreliable reference. A normative expectation for age, sex and weight is safer.

Practical takeaway: treat any single published number with suspicion, and build criteria from measurable data you collect the same way on every patient, using handheld dynamometry, inclinometer range of movement, endurance holds, a seated medicine ball throw and the PSFS.
