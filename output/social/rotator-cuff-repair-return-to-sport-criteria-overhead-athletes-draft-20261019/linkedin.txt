**A surgeon's protocol gives you a calendar. It does not tell you whether the athlete is ready.**

After rotator cuff repair, weeks since surgery sets the protective phases and the earliest point at which you may test. Once that window has passed, it stops being useful for deciding on throwing, serving or swimming volume.

Two athletes at the same post-operative stage can differ enormously in strength, endurance and tolerance of load. The 2022 Bern Consensus Statement supports staged, criteria-informed progression rather than elapsed time alone. High-quality trials of rotator cuff specific return-to-sport thresholds in overhead athletes are scarce, so the evidence is thinner than some protocols imply.

Where you cannot cite a threshold, do not invent one. Build criteria from measurable domains: inclinometer range of movement, dynamometer strength against a normative expectation, timed endurance holds and medicine ball throw distance. Track psychological readiness alongside, for example with the Patient-Specific Functional Scale.

Takeaway: treat the surgeon's timeline as a gate, let test results make the decision, and document your reasoning.
