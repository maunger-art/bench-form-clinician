The strength test you use to discharge a patient is also a health marker you're throwing away.

Quad force after a knee reconstruction, cuff torque after a repair, sit-to-stand time after a joint replacement: these numbers get measured against a discharge threshold, a box gets ticked, and the patient leaves. Read the same number over a longer horizon and it tells you something else entirely: whether that patient is building durable musculoskeletal capacity or just clearing a low bar.

The evidence base doesn't help. Rehab trials are built around 12-week outcomes and discharge criteria, and TKA rehab studies in particular vary so much in what they measure that pooling data into longitudinal benchmarks is nearly impossible.

Retest sit-to-stand time, grip strength or single-leg strength ratios at 3, 6 and 12 months post-discharge. It costs almost nothing and turns a one-off clearance test into a usable trajectory.
