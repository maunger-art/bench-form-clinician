[1/5] The quad strength test you use to discharge a knee patient can also predict their joint health years later. Most physios throw that signal away at discharge.

---

[2/5] After a TKA, quad strength deficits that persist beyond 'good enough for stairs' correlate with slower long-term joint loading tolerance, and a higher risk of the other knee breaking down under compensation.

---

[3/5] TKA rehab trials use wildly inconsistent outcome measures. That variation means nobody can currently pool data to say a strength trajectory at 6 months predicts joint health at 3 years.

---

[4/5] Sit-to-stand time, grip strength and single-leg strength ratios need no special kit and take minutes. Tracked at 3, 6 and 12 months post-discharge, they turn a one-off clearance test into a health record.

---

[5/5] Benchmark PS standardises the strength tests clinics already run, so the numbers stay comparable across patients, clinicians and time: https://benchmarkps.com/blog/posts/simple-strength-tests-predict-long-term-health/
