[1/5] Rotator cuff repair in an overhead athlete usually comes with a calendar, not a set of standards. Here is how to turn return-to-sport clearance into measurable criteria you can record and revisit.

---

[2/5] Two athletes at the same post-operative stage can have very different capacity. The calendar cannot tell you which one you are looking at. The Bern consensus (Schwank et al., 2022) frames return to sport as criteria-informed, not date-driven.

---

[3/5] Record baseline factors on day one: tear size, tendons involved, repair type, associated labral findings, sport, position, overhead demand and target level. A collegiate pitcher and a club swimmer with the same repair face different questions.

---

[4/5] You do not need an isokinetic machine. Handheld dynamometry in a standardised position gives repeatable strength data. A medicine ball throw with a tape measure gives a power score, once earlier criteria are met and the surgeon agrees.

---

[5/5] Agree with the surgeon which restrictions are fixed and which can progress on objective criteria, and write both down at the first appointment. Full article: https://benchmarkps.com/blog/posts/rotator-cuff-repair-overhead-athletes-return-to-sport-criteria-draft-20261116/
