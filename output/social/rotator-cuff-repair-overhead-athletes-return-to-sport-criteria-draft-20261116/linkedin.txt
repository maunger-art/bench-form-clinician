**Most overhead athletes are cleared after cuff repair because the calendar said so.**

A repair protocol gives you phases, and phases make sense for protecting healing tissue. The problem comes later. Two athletes at the same post-operative stage can have very different capacity, and the calendar cannot tell you which one you are looking at.

The Bern consensus statement on shoulder injury prevention, rehabilitation and return to sport describes return to sport as a structured, criteria-informed process that considers sport demands, not a single date or a single test (Schwank et al., 2022). Hamstring rehabilitation has already moved this way, replacing time-driven progression with explicit criteria (Mendiguchia et al., 2017).

In practice that means recording baseline factors from day one: tear size, tendons involved, repair type, associated labral or instability findings, sport, position, the specific overhead demand, and the level the athlete intends to return to. Then turn each into a number: range of movement, handheld dynamometry as a percentage of normative expectation, a medicine ball throw distance, and PSFS scores.

Practical takeaway: at the first appointment, agree with the surgeon which restrictions are fixed protection limits and which can progress on objective criteria. Write both down so the athlete knows what is being measured and why.
