Time-based clearance after rotator cuff repair sets overhead athletes up to fail.

Tendon-to-bone healing takes eight to twelve weeks before meaningful load, and that timeline doesn't move for a motivated athlete or an approaching season. But two athletes at sixteen weeks post-repair, with identical tissue healing, can have completely different capacity to tolerate overhead loading, because capacity is a function of strength, endurance and volume tolerance, not healing alone.

Prognosis is also set earlier than most rehab plans account for. Tear size and retraction, tendon and muscle quality, and the mismatch between age and demand all shape the ceiling on recovery before the first rehab session even starts.

Strength testing alone misses this. Peak strength can normalise while endurance and power still lag behind what the sport demands.

Use the healing timeline as a floor, not a clearance date. Build return-to-sport criteria around strength, endurance, power and psychological readiness instead.
