Time since surgery is a weak gate for returning an overhead athlete after rotator cuff repair.

Surgical protocols tell you when loading is permitted. They do not tell you whether the shoulder can tolerate a serve, a throw or a swing at speed. Two athletes at the same post-operative point can differ enormously in strength, range and confidence.

The 2022 Bern Consensus Statement frames return to sport as a continuum and a shared decision between clinician and athlete, informed by measured capacity rather than elapsed time. The same logic underpins criteria-based progression in other regions, such as the hamstring algorithm described by Mendiguchia and colleagues.

In clinic, that means capturing a precise baseline: inclinometer range in repeated positions, handheld dynamometer strength as a percentage of the normative expectation, and the Patient-Specific Functional Scale for the task the athlete actually needs, whether that is the serve or the throw. Medicine ball throw distance adds a simple measure of power.

Practical takeaway: write down the criteria for each phase before the athlete reaches it, and record the number, not a judgement of how it looked.
