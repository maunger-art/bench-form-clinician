[1/5] Lumbar radiculopathy referral decisions often get made on a bad week, not on data. Here are the objective checkpoints that should drive the call instead.

---

[2/5] Without a baseline at intake, "not improving" in week six is an impression, not a finding. Track PSFS, pain score, and strength against normative data from the first appointment.

---

[3/5] Lumbar spinal stenosis can look radicular, especially on a bad day, but its natural history and referral threshold differ. Running both conditions on the same clock skews the trial from the start.

---

[4/5] SIJ pain is often misread as radicular referral when a formal neuro exam isn't repeated to confirm a genuine dermatomal pattern. Get the diagnosis right before the trial clock starts.

---

[5/5] A defensible conservative trial needs an evidence-based technique, a baseline, and a scheduled re-test, not gut feel at week eight. Full breakdown: https://benchmarkps.com/blog/posts/continue-conservative-care-or-refer-on-lumbar-radiculopathy/
