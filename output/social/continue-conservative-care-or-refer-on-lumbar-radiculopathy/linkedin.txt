Referral decisions for lumbar radiculopathy are too often made on a bad week, not on data.

Most cases resolve with conservative care, which is exactly why the exceptions are hard to spot. Without a baseline captured at intake, "not improving" in week six is an impression, not a finding.

Two other conditions complicate the picture. Lumbar spinal stenosis can mimic radicular pain but carries a different natural history and referral threshold. Sacroiliac joint pain is frequently misread as radicular when a formal neurological exam isn't repeated to confirm the pattern.

A defensible conservative trial needs three things: an evidence-based technique, a baseline, and a scheduled re-test. Track PSFS, pain score against MCID, strength against normative data, and any progressive neurological signs.

Set the re-assessment point before the trial starts, not after the patient has lost confidence in it.
