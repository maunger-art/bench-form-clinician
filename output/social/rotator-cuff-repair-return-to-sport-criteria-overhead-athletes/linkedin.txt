Time since surgery tells you the date of the operation. It does not tell you whether an overhead athlete can throw, serve or swim.

Most rotator cuff rehabilitation still leans on the calendar once the surgeon's early restrictions end. The evidence base is small and heterogeneous, and shoulder return-to-sport research is thinner than the knee literature, where even two decades of criteria-based work has not settled which criteria predict outcome.

The 2022 Bern Consensus Statement frames return to sport as a staged process with the sport's demands in view. It is consensus, not trial evidence, so use it as a structure and fill it with measurements from your own patient: range, strength, endurance, power, plus PSFS and pain scores for their actual overhead tasks.

Two further points. Fixed factors such as tear size, tissue quality and surgical technique shape the conversation, so read the operation note. And agree at the outset whether you are aiming for return to participation, return to sport or return to previous performance.

Practical takeaway: replace a discharge date with gates you can measure, repeat the same test protocol each time, and name the source population when you quote expected outcomes.
