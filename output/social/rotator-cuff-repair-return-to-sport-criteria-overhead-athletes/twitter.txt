[1/5] Time since rotator cuff repair tells you the date of the operation. It does not tell you whether an overhead athlete can throw, serve or swim. Here is what to measure instead.

---

[2/5] Be honest about the evidence. Shoulder return-to-sport research is thinner than the knee, and even the knee field still argues about which criteria predict outcome. Quote outcomes cautiously and name the source population.

---

[3/5] Agree the target at the start: return to participation, return to sport, or return to previous performance. They are different decisions with different criteria.

---

[4/5] Low-cost tests work. Inclinometer for range, handheld dynamometer for strength, timed holds for endurance, medicine ball throws for power. Add PSFS and pain scores. Keep the protocol identical at every re-test.

---

[5/5] No validated universal cut-offs exist, but measurable gates are more defensible than a calendar. Full article: https://benchmarkps.com/blog/posts/rotator-cuff-repair-return-to-sport-criteria-overhead-athletes/
