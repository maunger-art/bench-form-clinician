[1/5] Strength gets measured. Range of movement gets measured. Power gets guessed. That judgement call is where a lot of rehab pathways lose their rigour.

---

[2/5] Reviews of post-surgical rehab protocols show strength and range of movement dominate outcome measures. Power rarely gets tracked at all, in knee, shoulder, and rotator cuff rehab alike.

---

[3/5] Power isn't strength done quickly. It's how fast the neuromuscular system produces force and how well it absorbs repeated load, exactly what landing and change of direction demand in sport.

---

[4/5] A drop jump measures this without a force plate. Time ground contact with a smartphone app, estimate jump height with a tape measure, divide the two for a reactive strength index you can track over sessions.

---

[5/5] Use it once basic strength and loading tolerance are cleared, not as a day-one screen. Full protocol: https://benchmarkps.com/blog/posts/power-testing-without-force-plate-drop-jump-in-clinic/
