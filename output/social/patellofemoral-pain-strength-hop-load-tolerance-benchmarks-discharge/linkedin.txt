A pain score under 2/10 is not a discharge criterion for patellofemoral pain.

PFP has one of the worst recurrence records in musculoskeletal practice, and a large part of the reason is how clinicians decide someone is ready to leave. Pain improves before strength and load tolerance catch up, so a patient can report feeling 90% better at six weeks while still carrying a 20-25% quadriceps deficit that only shows up under load, weeks after they've stopped attending.

Two patients with identical pain scores can have completely different underlying deficits. One with a 30% quad strength shortfall and normal hips, another with intact quads but a marked hip abductor and external rotation lag. Discharge both on symptoms alone and you never find out whether the mechanical driver of their pain was addressed.

Benchmark against quadriceps limb symmetry of 85-90%, hip abduction and external rotation strength within 15% of the uninvolved side (measured with handheld dynamometry, not manual muscle testing), plus hop and graduated load tolerance testing before signing off.
