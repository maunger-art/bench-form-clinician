[1/5] A pain score below 2/10 is not a discharge criterion for patellofemoral pain. It measures symptom tolerance, not tissue capacity, and that gap is a large part of why so many PFP patients relapse within a year.

---

[2/5] Pain improves before strength does. A patient can feel 90% better at six weeks while still carrying a 20-25% quad strength deficit that only shows up under load, after they've already stopped attending clinic.

---

[3/5] Two patients with identical pain scores can have opposite deficits: one 30% down on quad force with normal hips, another with intact quads but a hip abductor and external rotation lag. Symptom-based discharge treats them the same.

---

[4/5] A defensible discharge benchmark: quad limb symmetry index of 85-90%, hip abduction and external rotation strength within 15% of the uninvolved side using handheld dynamometry, plus hop and graduated load tolerance testing.

---
