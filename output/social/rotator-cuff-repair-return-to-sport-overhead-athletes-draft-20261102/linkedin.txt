Return to sport after rotator cuff repair should not be decided by the calendar.

Most post-operative protocols give approximate windows for motion, strengthening and sport-specific work. Those windows protect the repair, and surgeon restrictions on early loading should be respected. After that, the useful question is what this athlete can now do, not how many weeks have passed.

The 2022 Bern consensus frames return to sport as a continuum of decisions informed by athlete status, with shared decision-making between clinician, athlete and surgeon. Direct evidence in repaired cuffs in overhead athletes is limited, so much of this draws on consensus guidance and neighbouring shoulder conditions. Reviews of SLAP tears and anterior instability both note inconsistent reporting of return criteria and recommend objective measures over time alone.

Two athletes at the same post-operative week can differ enormously in strength, range and confidence. Inclinometer range, handheld dynamometry against normative data, endurance holds, medicine ball throw distance and PSFS are all low-cost and repeatable.

Takeaway: set your criteria before the athlete reaches the decision point, record what you measure, and progress only when the criteria are met.
